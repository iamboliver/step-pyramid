How to solve a Pyraminx

A beginner's method for the standard Pyraminx, in four stages. There are only four short move sequences to learn, and two of them are mirror images of each other.

Know the pieces

A standard Pyraminx has 14 pieces that move, in three kinds:

  • 4 tips, the small pyramids at the points. Each one twists on its own without disturbing anything else, which makes them the easiest part of the puzzle.
  • 4 corners, the larger three-coloured pieces just under the tips. They spin in place but never leave their point. Some guides call them centres.
  • 6 edges, the two-coloured pieces between the corners. They move all around the puzzle, so this is where the real solving happens.

Reading the moves

Hold the puzzle with one face towards you and one point straight up. The four points are named U (up), L (bottom left), R (bottom right) and B (the point at the back, facing away from you).

  • A capital letter on its own, like R, means turn that point a third of a turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that point. It turns everything except the face opposite that point.
  • A letter with an apostrophe, like R', means the same turn anticlockwise.
  • A lowercase letter, like r, means turn only the tip.

Keep holding the puzzle the same way all the way through a sequence. Only the layers move, not your grip.

Step 1Twist the tips

Twist each tip until its three colours match the corner just below it. A tip only has three positions, so this never takes more than two twists per tip.

The tips then stay matched for the rest of the solve, because every move in this guide turns a tip together with its corner.

Step 2Line up the corners

Next, turn the corners so that each face shows one colour in its three corner spots. Ignore the edges for now. They'll get scrambled while you do this, and that's fine.

  1. Find your bottom colour. The top corner shows three colours. The colour it's missing is your bottom colour.
  2. Turn the three bottom points. Turn L, R and B, each until that corner shows the bottom colour on the bottom face.
  3. Turn the top. Turn U until the top corner's front colour matches the two corners at the bottom of the front face.

Every face now has matching corners and tips.

Step 3Solve the first layer

Now put the three edges around the bottom face in place. Each one has your bottom colour plus one side colour.

  1. Pick an edge. Find an edge touching the top point that has the bottom colour. Its other colour tells you which face it belongs to. Turn the whole puzzle, keeping the same point up, until the face whose bottom corners are that colour is in front of you.
  2. Line it up. Turn U until the edge's side colour is on the front face. It will now be at the upper left or the upper right of the front face.
  3. Drop it in. Use the move for where it is. The edge drops into the bottom of the front face with both colours matching.

Repeat for the other two bottom edges. The top corner will turn as you go. That's expected, and you'll fix it in the next step.

If an edge is stuck at the bottom

If none of the edges touching the top point has your bottom colour, one is sitting in the wrong bottom spot or the wrong way round. Hold the puzzle so that edge is at the bottom of the front face, do RU'R' to lift it out, then carry on from the start of this step.

Step 4Solve the last layer

Only the three edges touching the top point are left.

1. Line up the top

Turn U until the top corner matches the faces again.

2. Move the edges into place

An edge is in the right place when its two colours match the two faces it sits between, even if they're the wrong way round. The top edges are either all in the right places or none of them are. If none are, do this sequence, which moves all three one place round:

RUR'URUR'

If they're still not in place, do it once more.

3. Flip the last two

Any edge that's the wrong way round now needs flipping. There are always either none or exactly two. Hold the puzzle so the correct edge is at the back, with the two flipped ones at the upper left and upper right of the front face, and do:

R'LRL'UL'U'L

That's it. Your Pyraminx is solved.

Last layer: two edges flipped, correct edge at the back R'LRL'UL'U'L

Getting faster

Once the method feels natural, try leaving the tips until the very end. They never affect anything else, so it saves looking at them twice. After that, speedcubing methods such as Keyhole and L4E solve more of the puzzle at once, in exchange for learning more sequences.

Common questions

Is there parity on the Pyraminx?
No. A Pyraminx that came from a solved state can always be finished with the moves above. If any top edges are flipped at the end, there will be exactly two.
Does it matter which point I hold at the top?
No. Any point works. Whichever one you choose decides your bottom colour, which is why step 2 starts by reading it off the top corner.
My edge won't go in. What am I doing wrong?
Usually one of three things: turning only the tip instead of the whole point, turning the wrong way (clockwise means as seen looking straight at that point), or changing your grip partway through a sequence.
Does this work for the 4-layer Master Pyraminx?
No. The Master Pyraminx has extra centre and wing pieces and needs a different method. The Step Pyramid solver handles it if you want a solution for your scramble.
